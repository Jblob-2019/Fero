Culinary Journeys & Traditions

Eat your way through Asia, plate by plate.

Every destination in Ferð begins at the dining table. Here is what makes the food and cultural rhythm of each place unforgettable.

🍜 Japan · Tokyo & Kyoto

The Art of Shokunin & Kaiseki

From 4am Tsukiji tuna auctions and lightning-fast standing ramen counters to Kyoto's multi-course seasonal Kaiseki banquets, eating in Japan is a spiritual devotion to craft and seasonal terroir.

Tonkotsu Ramen Edomae Sushi Kaiseki Matcha Sweets
🍛 Thailand · Bangkok

Canal-Side Spice & Street Stalls

Bangkok's culinary soul is found under the glow of neon market signs. Fiery tom yum soups, rich boat noodles simmered in dark aromatic spices, and coconut sticky rice with golden honey mangoes.

Boat Noodles Tom Yum Goong Pad Thai Mango Sticky Rice
🥥 India · Kerala

Spices, Coconut & Sadhya Feasts

The ancient spice coast serves fragrant feasts on banana leaves. Lace-edged rice appams dipped in rich coconut stew, fiery fish curries wrapped in banana leaves, and fragrant pepper plantations.

Appam & Stew Karimeen Pollichathu Onam Sadhya Kerala Parotta
🥘 South Korea · Seoul

Shared Bowls & Pocha Night Markets

Korean food is communal by nature. Sizzling stone-pot bibimbap, tables packed with fermenting banchan kimchi, and tented pocha stalls serving crisp pajeon pancakes and hot tteokbokki.

Dolsot Bibimbap Korean BBQ Kimchi Jjigae Tteokbokki
🍢 Vietnam · Hanoi

Plastic Stools & Simmering Broths

Hanoi mornings are defined by steamy pho aromas wafting down narrow French-colonial alleys, char-grilled pork patties sizzling over open coals for bun cha, and frothy egg coffee.

Pho Bo Bun Cha Egg Coffee Banh Mi
🌴 Indonesia · Bali

Island Herbs, Sambal & Warung Feasts

Balinese cuisine balances roasted spices (bumbu bali), crispy suckling pig (babi guling), fresh ocean catch grilled over coconut husks on Jimbaran beach, and fiery raw sambal matah.

Nasi Goreng Babi Guling Satay Lilit Sambal Matah
Local Customs & Table Etiquette

Respecting the table across cultures

Eating locally is one of the highest forms of cultural appreciation. Here are a few etiquette tips to remember when traveling.

🥢 Chopstick Etiquette (Japan & Vietnam)

Never stick chopsticks vertically into a bowl of rice (resembles funeral incense). Rest them horizontally on the ceramic holder provided.

✋ Right Hand Tradition (India & Bali)

In traditional banana-leaf dining, eat using the fingers of your right hand only. It is considered polite and connects you directly with the food texture.

🍶 Two-Handed Pouring (South Korea)

When pouring or receiving a drink or dish from someone older or senior, hold your glass or cup with both hands as a sign of deep respect.

🪙 Tipping Philosophy (Asia)

In Japan, tipping is not practiced and can even cause confusion. In Thailand and Vietnam, rounding up small change or leaving 5-10% at casual sit-downs is warmly appreciated.
